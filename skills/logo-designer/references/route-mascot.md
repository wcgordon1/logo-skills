# Mascot route

Use a mascot when personality, friendliness, trust, or recall should lead. A mascot is still a logo: it must survive simplification and small display.

## Choose a subroute

### Friendly-service mascot

Best for local services and approachable small businesses. Use a compact, capable character with one trade-relevant cue—for example, a beaver with a restrained plumbing detail. Signal competence before comedy.

### Iconic-venture mascot

Best for a startup seeking a character-led world with stronger attitude and broader future use. Use a more proprietary silhouette, less literal costuming, and one memorable behavioral trait. It may be cute, but cuteness is optional.

## Construction

- Start with species or character archetype, brand trait, and one distinctive silhouette move.
- Use a head, bust, or compact full-body pose; avoid a scene.
- Limit props and clothing to one meaningful cue.
- Keep facial features readable at 32 px.
- Prefer a stable outer contour and broad color masses.
- Check that the character does not resemble a well-known mascot.

## Prompt scaffold

```text
Create an original [friendly-service/iconic-venture] mascot logo for [brand/category].
Character: [archetype], expressing [two traits].
Distinctive structural move: [silhouette idea].
Pose/crop: [compact description].
Construction: bold silhouette, limited internal detail, logo-scale readability.
Color: [palette direction]. Background: plain.
Exclude: scenes, text unless explicitly required, extra props, costume clutter,
generic clip-art posture, and resemblance to known brand mascots.
```

## Repair failures

- Too illustrative: remove environment, secondary objects, texture, and small anatomy.
- Too childish: reduce eye size, smile intensity, and rounded proportions; strengthen posture.
- Too generic: alter the outer contour or character-object relationship, not merely color.
- Too busy: crop closer and preserve one expressive feature.
- Works only in 3D: return to a one-color silhouette before rerendering.
