# Transformed examples

These examples teach decisions and repairs; they do not prescribe a house style.

## 1. Friendly service mascot with optional 3D

**Request:** “Create a premium but cute beaver logo for a plumbing company. I also want the trendy 3D look.”

**Decision:** Choose the friendly-service mascot route. The concept thesis is “a compact, capable beaver whose tail forms a subtle pipe bend, signaling practical problem-solving.” Establish the tail-and-head silhouette in flat color first. Reject tool belts, bathroom scenes, and multiple plumbing props. After the flat mark passes, create a separate soft-3D enamel treatment while preserving the silhouette.

**Repair:** If the beaver becomes childish, reduce oversized eyes and smile intensity; retain a confident posture. If 3D changes the tail geometry, edit from the accepted flat mark and lock the contour.

## 2. Premium venture-backed abstract symbol

**Request:** “Make a polished logo for an AI operations startup—premium like Linear or Stripe, but original.”

**Decision:** Translate the references into restraint, geometric confidence, excellent spacing, and limited color—not signature geometry. Explore three separate theses: converging modular paths, a focused aperture, and an asymmetric continuous loop. Generate each independently in flat one-color form. Do not add gradients until the topology wins on its own.

**Repair:** If a result resembles a familiar SaaS ribbon, change its path topology, axis, proportion, and negative-space event. Recoloring does not create sufficient distance.

## 3. Monogram with exact initials

**Request:** “Design an AEJ monogram for AccountExecutiveJobs.com.”

**Decision:** Confirm the exact order `AEJ`. Explore shared-stem, nested-counter, and modular-interlock concepts separately. Image generation may suggest compositions, but every finalist must be rebuilt or verified as exact letter geometry. Test for accidental `AJ`, `AE`, or mirrored glyphs at small size.

## Anti-pattern: everything in one generation

**Bad approach:** Ask one model call for a mascot, abstract mark, object, monogram, emblem, wordmark, and six 3D materials in a labeled grid “like” five famous brands.

**Why it fails:** Concepts become tiny, prompts conflict, text degrades, reference imitation risk rises, and no result has an attributable revision path.

**Correction:** Frame the brief, select two or three defensible routes, write one thesis per concept, generate each in a separate flat-first call, apply hard gates, then render only the strongest one or two with an optional 3D treatment.
