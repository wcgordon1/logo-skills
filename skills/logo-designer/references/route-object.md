# Object or pictorial route

Use a concrete object when it can become a compact proprietary symbol. An object logo represents a recognizable noun; an abstract logo leads with nonliteral geometry.

## Selection

Choose an object that is:

- meaningful to the product, outcome, name, or customer—not merely category decoration
- recognizable from its outer contour
- uncommon enough to own, or transformed through an unusual structural move
- simple enough for a favicon or storefront mark

Avoid the most obvious category object unless the treatment creates a real distinction.

## Construction moves

- Combine two related meanings through negative space.
- Crop or rotate the object into an unexpected silhouette.
- Convert one functional feature into the brand's initial.
- Reduce the object to two or three decisive masses.
- Use a consistent geometric or organic construction language.

## Prompt scaffold

```text
Create an original pictorial logo for [brand/category] built around [single object].
Brand signal: [traits]. Distinctive structural move: [transformation].
Construction: [geometric/organic/modular/negative-space], bold outer contour,
two or three major shapes, readable at 24 px, flat vector-like master.
Exclude: a scene, multiple unrelated objects, clip-art detail, photorealism,
stock-icon composition, text unless requested, and category-cliche treatment.
```

## Repair failures

- Reads as an illustration: remove perspective, environment, texture, and tertiary detail.
- Reads as stock: change topology or negative space rather than adding decoration.
- Ambiguous at small size: enlarge the defining feature and simplify the contour.
- Becomes abstract accidentally: restore one unmistakable physical feature.
- Too literal: preserve recognition while introducing one meaningful visual transformation.
